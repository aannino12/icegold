The story

In the spring of 2001 the U.S. national sled hockey program was the worst in the world. It had qualified for the 2002 Paralympic Winter Games for one reason: the United States was the host.

USA Hockey turned to Rick Middleton, a three-time NHL All-Star and former Boston Bruins captain who had never watched a sled hockey game. Over one season he and assistant coach Tom Moulton took fifteen players from rival regional programs, taught them a system, and built a team.

Ice Gold follows that year through footage shot inside the locker room and on the ice in 2001 and 2002, paired with new interviews with Middleton, Moulton and the players, including goaltender Manny Guerra Jr., whose play decided the gold-medal game.

The win launched a dynasty. The U.S. men’s sled hockey team has since become the most dominant program in the sport, and the 2002 team was inducted into the U.S. Hockey Hall of Fame in 2024.

“I really didn’t know what sled hockey was.”Rick Middleton, head coach, on being offered the job
  1. 1998 · Nagano

    The U.S. fails to advance to the medal round at the Paralympic Winter Games.

  2. 2000 · World Championships

    The program finishes at the bottom of the world rankings. Qualification for 2002 comes only through host-nation status.

  3. 2001 · A new coach

    Rick Middleton accepts the head coaching job and begins uniting players from competing regional programs into one national team.

  4. 2001–02 · The season

    Camps, cuts, conflicts and a system the players have never run, captured on camera as it happens.

  5. March 2002 · Salt Lake City

    Playing at home, the United States goes undefeated and wins Paralympic gold.

  6. 2010–2022

    Four consecutive Paralympic gold medals follow. The 2002 team is inducted into the U.S. Hockey Hall of Fame in 2024.

  7. March 2026

    Ice Gold premieres on Peacock, presented by NBC Sports during the Milan Cortina Paralympic Winter Games.
